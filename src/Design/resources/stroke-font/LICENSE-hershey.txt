hershey-roman-simplex.txt — licence
===================================

The glyph coordinates in hershey-roman-simplex.txt (in this folder) and in
../silkscreen-glyphs/hershey-variants.txt are glyphs of the Hershey fonts, converted into circuitRF's
own format from the Usenet distribution of the fonts. The Hershey fonts are in the public domain. That
distribution may be used by anyone for any purpose, commercial or otherwise, provided that the
following acknowledgements are distributed with the font data:

  - The Hershey Fonts were originally created by Dr. A. V. Hershey while working at the U. S.
    National Bureau of Standards.
  - The format of the Font data in this distribution was originally created by
        James Hurt
        Cognition, Inc.
        900 Technology Park Drive
        Billerica, MA 01821

and that the font data is not converted into the format distributed by the U.S. National Technical
Information Service (NTIS). Neither file is in that format.

What the files hold:

  - hershey-roman-simplex.txt: the Roman Simplex set of the distribution for every printable ASCII
    character (U+0020 to U+007E), and four glyphs beyond it — the Omega (Hershey 550) and the mu
    (Hershey 638, standing for the micro sign) of the simplex Greek set, the simplex degree mark
    (Hershey 718), and the plus-minus of the simplex mathematical symbols.
  - ../silkscreen-glyphs/hershey-variants.txt: three further glyphs (two 1s and a 3) assembled from the
    strokes of the Hershey Roman Simplex glyphs and the Hershey serif Roman face, covered by this
    licence in the same way.

How circuitRF uses them:

  - hershey-roman-simplex.txt is the stroke font circuitRF draws layout labels in — on the layout
    canvas, in rendered pictures (SVG, PDF, PNG), and as pen strokes in a Gerber export — and it
    supplies the matching templates Create Schematic from Artwork reads stroked silkscreen
    designators against.
  - hershey-variants.txt holds matching templates only: nothing is drawn with those three glyphs.
